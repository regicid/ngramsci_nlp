    How the scores are made

    Le Monde, 1944 to November 2026 (3.2 million articles, every section), and the politics sections of 32 French news sites, 2008 to November 2026 (1.48 million articles; the section comes from n-gramsci/rubriques-presse, and from the same classifier after June 2026). People are found by named-entity recognition and Qwen coreference. Each passage centred on a mention is scored by portrayal-camembert-large v2 (seed 0), trained on codebook v4.

    Valence is P(positive) − P(negative) judgement of the person. Radical framing is P(framed as radical or extreme) − P(framed as moderate). Momentum is P(rising in the race) − P(declining). All three run from −1 to +1. Most passages carry no judgement, so averages sit close to 0.

    A point averages every scored passage, weighted by the probability that the passage says something about the person. Long portraits weigh more than passing mentions. Shaded bands and whiskers are 95% intervals, with articles as the resampling unit.

    A person is named by the full name in their coreference chain. A chain that only says « Mélenchon » or « M. Mélenchon » takes the article's own « Jean-Luc Mélenchon » when there is exactly one; otherwise the person that family name stands for that year, when one person holds at least 90% of the articles naming someone « … Mélenchon ». Le Pen, place names, and family names that are also first names (Philippe, Bertrand) are never resolved that way. A name groups every spelling that differs only by case, accents or hyphens, and homonyms are merged. Only names found in at least MIN_DOCS articles are listed. The mix of newspapers changes over the years (only Le Monde before 2008). Pick a fixed set of newspapers to compare years on equal terms.
